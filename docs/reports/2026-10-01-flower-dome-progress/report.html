AISEE-BIN: Flower Dome progress

Status update for the team · 1 October 2026 · Gardens by the Bay, Flower Dome POC

The goal: guide blind and low-vision visitors along the Flower Dome tour (Point A, through Doors B and C, to Points 1–4) with spoken directions. Guidance comes from a phone or from AiSee glasses.

1Scan the place
Walk the route once with a phone so it can learn what the place looks like.
2Add the route
In our web editor, draw the walkways and add the stops, hazards and what to say at each.
3Guide the visitor
The phone or glasses recognise where they are and give spoken directions and announcements.

Two ways to scan, and the best of both

A · Our app scans and guides (ARKit)

The iPhone's own tracking, scanned in our app's Author mode
  • + One walk gives the map, the route and the stops
  • + Smooth, continuous tracking. Worked inside the dome
  • − iPhone only, and not for glasses
  • − Struggled at the entrance, and sensitive to light

B · Immersal scans, our app guides

Scanned with the Immersal Mapper app; route added in our editor
  • + Found the entrance instantly
  • + Works for phones and glasses, on iOS and Android
  • − About 500 photos per map, so the full route needs several scans
  • − Joining scans into one map is unsolved
Best of both: Immersal tells the phone where it is, and ARKit tracks every step in between. The phone already does this on Immersal maps. Next we use it to extend maps and to join scans.

1 · The two ways in detail

A · Our app scans (ARKit)B · Immersal scans, our app guides
How a map is madeOpen Author mode, name the place, walk the route, mark each stop, then Publish. The route comes out of the walk itself.Scan with the Immersal Mapper app. Then Import from Immersal in our editor and draw the route and stops on top of the scan.
How it finds youThe phone recognises the place from its saved world map (about 26 MB for the Flower Dome walk). It then tracks every step.A camera frame is matched against the Immersal map and gives a position fix. Between fixes, the phone tracks with ARKit and the glasses count steps.
DevicesiPhone only.iPhone, Android and AiSee glasses.
Strengths
  • Quick to make: one walk.
  • Very smooth tracking.
  • Works offline once downloaded.
  • Announcements arrived just before each point.
  • Recognises busy, repetitive places better (the entrance).
  • Can match online or on the phone itself.
  • The only option for the glasses.
Weaknesses
  • Recognition fails with changing light or look-alike surfaces.
  • Recognises best near where the scan started.
  • Two scans can't be joined, and a map can't be extended yet.
  • About 500 photos per map.
  • A large site needs several scans, and lining them up by hand was out by tens of metres.
  • The scanning and the route-making happen in two different tools.

Combining them

Immersal gives an occasional, absolute "you are here", and ARKit gives smooth, step-by-step tracking that drifts slowly. Together they give the strengths of both. Today the phone already uses ARKit between Immersal fixes. Three next steps:

  • Add to an existing map. Immersal places the phone in the map first, so a new walk lands in the same coordinates and extends the map instead of starting a new one.
  • Join Immersal scans. A walk that sees two scans within a few seconds tells us exactly how they fit together. That is the "link scans" step of the on-site test; see section 4.
  • Author-mode Immersal maps. Our Author mode can also send photos to Immersal as it scans (up to 150), so one walk gives both kinds of map. On 1 October no Immersal map came out of it; still to check.

2 · Matching online or on the device

With Immersal maps, a camera frame can be matched by Immersal's server or by the phone itself. The app has a switch with three settings: Auto, On phone and Immersal server.

Online (Immersal server)On the device
Pros
  • Nothing to download.
  • Always uses the latest map.
  • Light on the phone's processor and battery.
  • Works with no internet. Glasses use their own Wi-Fi, so the phone has no internet while streaming unless it has mobile data.
  • Fast with one map: about 0.3 s per fix on our Android test phone.
  • Camera frames never leave the phone.
Cons
  • Needs mobile data.
  • About 1–3 s per try on site, sometimes up to 8 s.
  • Requests timed out on 1 October.
  • The map must be downloaded first.
  • Slower when several maps are loaded: 1.5–8 s per try with the four Flower Dome scans on an iPhone 13 Pro Max.
  • Slower on older phones.
  • One iPhone issue still to fix.
Best forPhones with good mobile data.The glasses, and places with poor signal. Ideally one map per area.

3 · The web map editor (aiseebin.flowsxr.com)

What you can do

  • Open any map from the Map menu. Each saved change becomes a new version, listed on the left, so nothing is lost.
  • Import from Immersal to start a map from an Immersal scan.
  • See the scan as a point cloud, top-down or in 3D, to tell where walls, paths and beds are.
  • Line up several Immersal scans into one map (Immersal maps panel).
  • Download the map files (route data, world map, point cloud).

How to add or edit a stop

  1. + Node and click on the map; drag to move it.
  2. Set the Type:
    • Exhibit: a stop that is announced in passing.
    • Hazard: a warning, with a vibration.
    • Destination: a place visitors can ask to go to.
    • Junction: a routing point only.
  3. Write the Spoken description, add Aliases (other names visitors might say), and set how close to announce it.
  4. Connect nodes (or draw a Path) to make walkways.
  5. Type what changed and click Save as new version. The apps pick it up the next time they open the map.

The glasses have no ARKit, so they rely on Immersal maps (Way B). The phone stays in a pocket and does the work.

6 · What we learned

  • Light matters for both ways. Tours run in the daytime, so scans should be made in daylight.
  • The entrance is the hard part. It has look-alike surfaces, graphics on the barriers and changing light. Immersal handled it better than ARKit.
  • One map is far easier than many. Lining up scans by hand was out by tens of metres, and automatic matching is unreliable on plants. Scanning the whole route as one Immersal map is the quickest thing to try.
  • Remote testing works. Field Test logs and the editor let us diagnose problems while our tester was on site.
